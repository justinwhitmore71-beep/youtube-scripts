Script A

Shape, not metal

the wrong guess is 'steel always sinks'
85 words, about 38 s

1[HOOK] A steel bolt sinks, but a huge ship of the same steel floats.

2[MASCOT: Steel should sink!] Shouldn't steel sink straight to the bottom?

3 Water pushes up with a force equal to the weight of water pushed aside.

4[EQUATION: F_buoyant = weight of water pushed aside] A steel lump pushes aside a little water, so it sinks.

5 Shape it into a hollow hull, and it pushes aside far more water at the same weight.

6 When the pushed-aside water matches the ship's weight, it floats.

7[TAKEAWAY] Ships float by shape: steel plus air averages out lighter than water.

Script B

Ball it, fold it

the same foil sinks or floats
83 words, about 37 s

1[HOOK] Squash foil into a tight ball and it sinks; fold it into a tray and it floats.

2[MASCOT: Same foil, how?] Same foil, same weight: how?

3[EQUATION: F_buoyant = weight of water pushed aside] Water pushes up with a force equal to the weight of the water you push aside.

4 The ball pushes aside only its own small volume.

5 The tray pushes aside a big hollow volume, so the push up grows.

6 It settles once it has pushed aside its own weight of water.

7[TAKEAWAY] That's how steel ships float: the shape, not the metal, decides.
