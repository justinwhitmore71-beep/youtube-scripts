## Description
Water pushes up on anything in it with a force equal to the weight of the water it pushes aside (Archimedes' principle). A solid steel bolt pushes aside a little water and sinks; the same steel shaped into a hollow hull pushes aside far more water at the same weight, so the push up can match the ship's weight and it floats.

## Pinned comment (suggested)
A ship floats because steel PLUS all the air inside the hull is, on average, less dense than water. Let water into the hull and the average density goes up, which is why a flooded ship sinks.
