# Brief: why any number to the power zero is 1

Series: Simple Proofs
Idea: Dividing a power by itself gives 1 (every copy cancels), but the subtraction rule for dividing powers gives exponent 0, so a^0 must equal 1; walking down the powers (8, 4, 2, ...) by dividing lands on 1 too.
Misconception: A number to the power zero should be zero: zero copies multiplied together is nothing.
Hook angle: Any number to the power zero is one, and dividing proves it.
Narrowed to: Whole-number powers of a nonzero number. No negative or fractional exponents, no 0^0.
Key condition to say aloud: The base must not be zero (0^0 is left undefined in the course textbooks).
Candidate sources: OpenStax, Algebra and Trigonometry 2e, §1.2 Exponents and Scientific Notation (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-algebra-bundle/blob/main/modules/m51241/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Calculus Volume 1, §1.5 Exponential and Logarithmic Functions (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-calculus-bundle/blob/main/modules/m53481/index.cnxml)
Originality: Algebra and Trigonometry 2e uses the same t^8/t^8 argument in symbols; ours does it with five cubed shown as real copies that visibly cancel, and B walks down 2^3, 2^2, 2^1 as a staircase.
Not used: 0^0 (debated / undefined depending on context; only one source opened says 'undefined'); negative exponents (the staircase continues to 1/2, 1/4: a nice follow-up Short).
