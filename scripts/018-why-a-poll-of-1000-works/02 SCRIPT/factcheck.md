# Fact-check: why a poll of 1,000 people works for a whole country (both scripts)

| # | Claim | Source 1 | Source 2 | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| c1 | For a random sample, the accuracy of the estimate depends on the sample size, not on the population size: 1,000 can represent a population of 100,000 or 1,000,000 equally well. | [OpenStax, Introductory Statistics 2e, §1.2 Data, Sampling, and Variation in Data and Sampling (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-introductory-statistics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m46885/index.cnxml): "It may be somewhat counterintuitive that the sample size does not depend on the population size. However, this implies that a sample size of 1,000 can be adequate to represent a population of 100,000 versus 1,000,000 given that the same level of precision is desired." | [Adhikari, DeNero & Wagner, Computational and Inferential Thinking (UC Berkeley Data 8), §14.5 Variability of the Sample Mean](https://github.com/data-8/textbook/blob/main/chapters/14/5/Variability_of_the_Sample_Mean.ipynb): "The population size doesn't affect the accuracy of the sample mean. The population size doesn't appear anywhere in the formula." | OK |
| c2 | With 1,000 people, the 95% margin of error for a percentage is at most about 3 percentage points. | [OpenIntro, Introduction to Modern Statistics (Çetinkaya-Rundel & Hardin), Inference for a single proportion](https://github.com/OpenIntroStat/ims/blob/main/inference-one-prop.qmd): "The margin of error is $z^star times sqrt{frac{ hat{p}(1-hat{p}) }{n}}$" | [Adhikari, DeNero & Wagner, Computational and Inferential Thinking (UC Berkeley Data 8), §14.6 Choosing a Sample Size](https://github.com/data-8/textbook/blob/main/chapters/14/6/Choosing_a_Sample_Size.ipynb): "The SD of a population of 1's and 0's is at most 0.5." | OK |
| c3 | The typical error of a sample estimate shrinks with the square root of the sample size. | [Adhikari, DeNero & Wagner, Computational and Inferential Thinking (UC Berkeley Data 8), §14.5 Variability of the Sample Mean](https://github.com/data-8/textbook/blob/main/chapters/14/5/Variability_of_the_Sample_Mean.ipynb): "with an SD equal to the population SD divided by the square root of the sample size." | [OpenStax, Introductory Statistics 2e, §8.3 A Population Proportion (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-introductory-statistics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m46999/index.cnxml): "The error bound for a proportion is EBP = ( z α 2 ) ( p ′ q ′ n )" | OK |

Notes: c2 derived: margin ≈ z × SD / √n with z ≈ 2 for 95% (Data 8 §14.6 uses 'center ± 2 SDs') and SD at most 0.5 for a yes/no question: 2 × 0.5 / √1000 = 0.0316, about 3 percentage points. With z = 1.96 and p = 0.5: 1.96 × √(0.25/1000) = 0.031. The IMS quote is from the formula line (z*, square root of p̂(1 − p̂)/n), with LaTeX backslashes removed. Assumes a simple random sample from a population much larger than 1,000 (Data 8 §14.6 states this assumption).
