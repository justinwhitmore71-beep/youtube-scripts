# Brief: why a poll of 1,000 people works for a whole country

Series: Brain Breakers
Idea: A random sample's typical error depends on the sample size (it shrinks like 1/√n), not on the population size, so 1,000 random people give about ±3 percentage points for a city or a country alike.
Misconception: A bigger country needs a much bigger poll; 1,000 out of millions can't mean anything.
Hook angle: Ask one thousand random people, and you can gauge a whole country within about three percent.
Narrowed to: A simple random sample estimating one percentage, about 95% confidence. No weighting, no non-response, no real elections.
Key condition to say aloud: The sample must be random (each person equally likely to be picked) and the population much bigger than the sample; '±3%' holds about 95% of the time.
Candidate sources: OpenStax, Introductory Statistics 2e, §1.2 Data, Sampling, and Variation in Data and Sampling (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-introductory-statistics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m46885/index.cnxml); Adhikari, DeNero & Wagner, Computational and Inferential Thinking (UC Berkeley Data 8), §14.5 Variability of the Sample Mean (https://github.com/data-8/textbook/blob/main/chapters/14/5/Variability_of_the_Sample_Mean.ipynb); Adhikari, DeNero & Wagner, Computational and Inferential Thinking (UC Berkeley Data 8), §14.6 Choosing a Sample Size (https://github.com/data-8/textbook/blob/main/chapters/14/6/Choosing_a_Sample_Size.ipynb); OpenIntro, Introduction to Modern Statistics (Çetinkaya-Rundel & Hardin), Inference for a single proportion (https://github.com/OpenIntroStat/ims/blob/main/inference-one-prop.qmd); OpenStax, Introductory Statistics 2e, §8.3 A Population Proportion (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-introductory-statistics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m46999/index.cnxml)
Originality: Usually explained with a soup-tasting analogy. Ours uses a jar of marbles at two sizes side by side (B) and puts the √n on screen, so the population size visibly never appears.
Not used: Real poll misses (biased samples, non-response: true but a different Short); exact 95% z = 1.96 (rounded to 2 on screen, as in Data 8).
