## Description
Warm a solid and it grows slightly: ΔL = αLΔT. For steel, α is only 12 millionths per degree, but a 1,275 m steel span warming from −15 °C to 40 °C grows by 12 × 10⁻⁶ × 1275 × 55 ≈ 0.84 m. Expansion joints let bridges change length like this without buckling. (The numbers are the textbook example of an all-steel span.)

## Pinned comment (suggested)
The 84 cm is the textbook calculation for a 1,275 m all-steel span over a 55 °C range; real bridges spread that movement over several joints, so each gap moves much less. It's also why railway tracks and pavements have gaps.
