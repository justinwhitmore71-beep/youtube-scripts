# Brief: why you can't divide by zero

Series: Simple Proofs
Idea: Division undoes multiplication, so 10 ÷ 0 asks 'what times 0 makes 10?', and since anything times 0 is 0, no number works; 'infinity' fails too, because dividing by tiny negative numbers sends the answers the other way.
Misconception: Dividing by zero gives infinity (or zero); the 'undefined' rule is just a teacher's ban.
Hook angle: Ten divided by zero isn't infinity; it has no answer at all.
Narrowed to: Real numbers only. No 0 ÷ 0 (mentioned in the pinned comment), no extended number systems.
Key condition to say aloud: Ordinary real numbers (some advanced systems handle infinity differently).
Candidate sources: OpenStax, Prealgebra 2e, §1.4 Divide Whole Numbers (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-prealgebra-bundle/blob/main/modules/m81256/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Elementary Algebra 2e, §1.8 Properties of Real Numbers (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-prealgebra-bundle/blob/main/modules/m82460/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Calculus Volume 1, §2.2 The Limit of a Function (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-calculus-bundle/blob/main/modules/m53491/index.cnxml); APEX Calculus, §1.6 Limits Involving Infinity (Hartman et al., Virginia Military Institute) (https://github.com/APEXCalculus/APEXCalculus_Source/blob/master/text/01_Limits_Involving_Infinity.tex)
Originality: Usually stated as a rule. Ours asks the reverse question (what times zero makes ten?) and then knocks out 'infinity' with the two-sided graph of 1/x.
Not used: 0 ÷ 0 ('every number works', also undefined); calculator error messages; extended real line / Riemann sphere (beyond the audience).
