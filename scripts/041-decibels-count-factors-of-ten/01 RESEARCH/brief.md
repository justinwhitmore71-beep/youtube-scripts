# Brief: why 90 dB is a thousand times 60 dB

Series: One Equation
Idea: The decibel level is β = 10 log₁₀(I/I₀): it counts factors of ten in intensity, so every extra 10 dB is ten times the intensity, 90 dB vs 60 dB is 10 × 10 × 10 = 1000 times, and doubling the intensity adds only about 3 dB.
Misconception: Decibels work like an ordinary scale: 90 dB is just 50% more than 60 dB, and double the sound is double the decibels.
Hook angle: Turn a sound up from sixty decibels to ninety: that's a thousand times the intensity.
Narrowed to: Sound intensity level only. No perceived loudness (how loud it seems is a different question), no hearing damage thresholds.
Key condition to say aloud: 'Intensity' means the sound's power per square meter, not how loud it seems to a listener.
Candidate sources: OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §17.3 Sound Intensity and Sound Level (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42257/index.cnxml); OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §17.3 Sound Intensity (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58377/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Algebra and Trigonometry 2e, §6.6 Exponential and Logarithmic Equations (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-algebra-bundle/blob/main/modules/m49366/index.cnxml); OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §16.11 Energy in Waves: Intensity (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42250/index.cnxml); OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §16.4 Energy and Power of a Wave (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58371/index.cnxml)
Originality: Usually given as a table of everyday sounds. Ours builds β = 10 log(I/I₀) and shows it counting tens, with the three tens between 60 and 90 dB stacked on screen.
Not used: Perceived loudness ('twice as loud per 10 dB' is often quoted but not found in two sources here); safe-exposure limits (University Physics quotes agencies' 85 dB guideline; one source).
