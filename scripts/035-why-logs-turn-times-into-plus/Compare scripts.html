Logs are exponents

the wrong guess is 'log of a product is a product of logs'
82 words, about 36 s

1[HOOK] Logarithms turn every multiplication into an addition.

2[MASCOT: Isn't it log a times log b?] Shouldn't the log of a product be a product of logs?

3 A log answers one question: what power of ten gives this number?

4 So log of a hundred is two, and log of a thousand is three.

5[EQUATION: 10² × 10³ = 10⁵ so log(100 × 1000) = 2 + 3] A hundred times a thousand is ten squared times ten cubed: ten to the five.

6 Multiplying powers adds the exponents, so the logs add: two plus three is five.

7[TAKEAWAY] Logs are exponents, so they turn times into plus.

Script B

Counting zeros

for powers of ten, the log counts the zeros
82 words, about 36 s

1[HOOK] The log of a product is the sum of the logs, not their product.

2[MASCOT: That breaks the pattern!] Why plus, when the numbers are multiplied?

3 For powers of ten, the log is just the number of zeros: a thousand has three.

4[EQUATION: log(M × N) = log M + log N] A thousand times a hundred is a hundred thousand: five zeros.

5 Three zeros plus two zeros makes five, because multiplying stacks the zeros together.

6 That rule holds for all positive numbers, not just powers of ten.

7[TAKEAWAY] Logs count tens multiplied together, so multiplying adds logs.
