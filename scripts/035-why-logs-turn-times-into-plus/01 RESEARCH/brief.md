# Brief: why logarithms turn multiplication into addition

Series: Simple Proofs
Idea: A logarithm is an exponent (log of 1000 is 3 because 10³ = 1000), and multiplying powers of the same base adds the exponents (10² × 10³ = 10⁵), so the log of a product is the sum of the logs.
Misconception: The log of a product should be the product of the logs.
Hook angle: Logarithms turn every multiplication into an addition.
Narrowed to: Base-10 logs, shown with powers of ten; the general rule log(MN) = log M + log N stated for positive numbers. No natural logs, no change of base.
Key condition to say aloud: Positive numbers only (logs of zero and negatives aren't defined).
Candidate sources: OpenStax, Algebra and Trigonometry 2e, §6.3 Logarithmic Functions (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-algebra-bundle/blob/main/modules/m49363/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Intermediate Algebra 2e, §10.4 Use the Properties of Logarithms (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-prealgebra-bundle/blob/main/modules/m81411/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Algebra and Trigonometry 2e, §6.5 Logarithmic Properties (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-algebra-bundle/blob/main/modules/m49365/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Algebra and Trigonometry 2e, §1.2 Exponents and Scientific Notation (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-algebra-bundle/blob/main/modules/m51241/index.cnxml)
Originality: Usually taught as a rule to memorise. Ours shows WHY with zeros: a hundred times a thousand stacks two zeros and three zeros, and that stacking IS the addition.
Not used: Slide rules and the history of logarithms (not sourced here); natural logs and e.
