# Brief: why multiplying by i is a quarter turn

Series: Simple Proofs
Idea: On the complex plane, multiplying by i turns every point a quarter turn (90 degrees) around zero, so doing it twice is a half turn, which is exactly what multiplying by minus one does; that is why i times i is minus one.
Misconception: i is a fake, made-up number that breaks the rules, because no number squared can be negative.
Hook angle: i times i equals minus one sounds impossible, until you see that multiplying by i is a quarter turn.
Narrowed to: Multiplication by i only, shown on the complex plane. No polar form on screen, no Euler's formula, no history.
Key condition to say aloud: Ordinary (real) numbers run left to right and imaginary numbers run up and down; the turn is counterclockwise.
