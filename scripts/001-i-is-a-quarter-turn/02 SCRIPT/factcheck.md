| c3 | Multiplying complex numbers multiplies their distances from zero and adds their angles; i is one unit straight up (angle 90 degrees), so multiplying by i turns any point a quarter turn (90 degrees) without changing its distance. | [OpenStax, Algebra and Trigonometry 2e, §10.5 Polar Form of Complex Numbers (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-algebra-bundle/blob/main/modules/m49408/index.cnxml): "Notice that the product calls for multiplying the moduli and adding the angles." | [Judson, Abstract Algebra: Theory and Applications, §4.2 The Multiplicative Group of Complex Numbers (Stephen F. | c4 | Worked examples: -1 times (a + bi) = -a - bi, the point straight through zero on the other side (a half turn); i(2 + i) = 2i + i squared = -1 + 2i: the point (2, 1) moves to (-1, 2), same distance from zero and at right angles to where it was. | Proven on screen: (-1)(a + bi) = -a - bi reflects (a, b) through 0, a half turn. i(2 + i) = 2i + i^2 = -1 + 2i using c1. Lengths: 2 + i and -1 + 2i are both √5 from zero. Right angle: dot product (2)(-1) + (1)(2) = 0. Swap-and-flip: i(a + bi) = -b + ai, i.e. (a, b) → (-b, a). | - | PROOF |

Notes: c3 is what makes 'i is a quarter turn' general: |i| = 1 and arg(i) = 90 degrees, so multiplying by i adds 90 degrees and keeps lengths. Both scripts only animate examples (A: 1 → i → -1; B: 2 + i → -1 + 2i).
