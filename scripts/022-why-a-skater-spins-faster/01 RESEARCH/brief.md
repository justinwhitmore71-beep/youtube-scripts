# Brief: why a skater spins faster with arms in

Series: Everyday Physics
Idea: On ice almost nothing twists a spinning skater, so their angular momentum L = Iω stays fixed; pulling the arms in shrinks I (mass counts by distance squared), so the spin rate ω must rise.
Misconception: To spin faster the skater must push off the ice harder.
Hook angle: A spinning skater speeds up just by pulling their arms in.
Narrowed to: One skater, arms out to arms in, ice friction ignored. No vectors, no gyroscopes.
Key condition to say aloud: Almost no outside twist (torque): the ice exerts very little, close to the spin axis.
Candidate sources: OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §10.5 Angular Momentum and Its Conservation (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42182/index.cnxml); OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §11.3 Conservation of Angular Momentum (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58337/index.cnxml); OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §10.3 Dynamics of Rotational Motion: Rotational Inertia (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42179/index.cnxml); OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §10.4 Moment of Inertia and Rotational Kinetic Energy (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58329/index.cnxml)
Originality: Usually shown as a video clip with 'conservation of angular momentum' as the answer. Ours builds L = I × ω and shows WHY I drops so much: arms at half the distance count a quarter. B answers the follow-up nobody asks: where the extra energy comes from.
Not used: The textbook's worked numbers (0.800 rev/s to about 5 rev/s, in one source only); spinning stars and the solar system (one source each here).
