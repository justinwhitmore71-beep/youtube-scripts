# Fact-check: why a skater spins faster with arms in (both scripts)

| # | Claim | Source 1 | Source 2 | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| c1 | On ice the twisting effect (torque) on a spinning skater is almost zero, so their angular momentum ('amount of spin') L = Iω stays constant. | [OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §10.5 Angular Momentum and Its Conservation (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42182/index.cnxml): "The net torque on her is very close to zero, because there is relatively little friction between her skates and the ice and because the friction is exerted very close to the pivot point." | [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §11.3 Conservation of Angular Momentum (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58337/index.cnxml): "Her angular momentum is conserved because the net torque on her is negligibly small." | OK |
| c2 | The moment of inertia I adds up each bit of mass times its distance from the axis squared (I = Σmr²), so mass farther out counts much more; at half the distance a mass counts a quarter as much. | [OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §10.3 Dynamics of Rotational Motion: Rotational Inertia (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42179/index.cnxml): "we define the moment of inertia I of an object to be the sum of mr2 for all the point masses of which it is composed." | [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §10.4 Moment of Inertia and Rotational Kinetic Energy (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58329/index.cnxml): "the moment of inertia of a single point particle about a fixed axis is simply mr2, with r being the distance from the point particle to the axis of rotation." | OK |
| c3 | Pulling the arms in reduces I, so the spin rate ω must increase to keep L = Iω constant. | [OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §10.5 Angular Momentum and Its Conservation (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42182/index.cnxml): "Because I ′ is smaller, the angular velocity ω ′ must increase to keep the angular momentum constant." | [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §11.3 Conservation of Angular Momentum (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58337/index.cnxml): "Because I ′ is smaller, the angular velocity ω ′ must increase to keep the angular momentum constant." | OK |
| c4 | The extra rotational kinetic energy comes from the work the skater does pulling the arms in. | [OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §10.5 Angular Momentum and Its Conservation (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42182/index.cnxml): "The work she does to pull in her arms results in an increase in rotational kinetic energy." | [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §11.3 Conservation of Angular Momentum (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58337/index.cnxml): "The source of this additional rotational kinetic energy is the work required to pull her arms inward." | OK |

Notes: Both are OpenStax books (College Physics 2e and University Physics Vol. 1, different author teams); University Physics reuses some of College Physics' wording (c3 is word-for-word the same sentence), so Justin may want a non-OpenStax second source. c2 'a quarter': m(r/2)² = ¼mr². College Physics §10.5 has a worked example (60 kg skater, 0.800 rev/s with I = 2.34 kg·m² to I = 0.363 kg·m², giving about 5.16 rev/s), not used because it is in one source.
