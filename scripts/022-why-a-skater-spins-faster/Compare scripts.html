1[HOOK] A spinning skater speeds up just by pulling their arms in.

2[MASCOT: They must push off harder!] Surely they're pushing off the ice harder?

3 Smooth ice barely twists them, so their amount of spin, L, stays fixed.

4[EQUATION: L = I × ω] Spin is I, how spread out the mass is, times omega, the turning speed.

5 Mass counts by distance squared, so arms pulled halfway in count a quarter.

Script B

Who pays for the extra spin?

the energy comes from the arms
83 words, about 37 s

1[HOOK] A skater spins much faster the moment their arms come in, with no push.

2[MASCOT: Where does the extra energy come from?] Then where's the extra energy from?

3 On ice almost nothing twists them, so their amount of spin stays fixed.

4[EQUATION: L = I × ω] That amount is I, how spread out the mass is, times omega, turning speed.

5 Arms in, I shrinks, so omega must climb to compensate.

6 Pulling the arms in takes work, and that becomes the extra spin energy.

7[TAKEAWAY] Pull mass inward and the spin speeds up; your muscles pay the energy.
