# Fact-check: add one meter to a rope around Earth (both scripts)

| # | Claim | Source 1 | Source 2 | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| c1 | A circle's circumference (length around) is 2π times its radius: C = 2πr, for any size of circle. | [OpenStax, Contemporary Mathematics, §10.4 Polygons, Perimeter, and Circumference (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-contemporary-mathematics/blob/main/modules/m00128/index.cnxml): "To find the circumference, we use the formula C=πd, where d is the diameter, the distance across the center, or C=2πr, where r is the radius." | [OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §25.7 The Formation of the Galaxy (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59955/index.cnxml): "Remember that the circumference of a circle is given by 2πR, where R is the radius of the circle." | OK |
| c2 | Earth's radius is about 6,371 km, so a rope around the equator is about 40,000 km long. | [OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §21.5 Planets beyond the Solar System: Search and Discovery (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59921/index.cnxml): "The radius of Earth is 6371 km." | [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §9.6 Center of Mass (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58323/index.cnxml): "The radius of Earth is 6.37 × 106m" | OK |
| c3 | Adding 1 m to the rope adds 1/(2π) m ≈ 0.16 m (16 cm) to its radius, whatever the circle's size, Earth or basketball. | [OpenStax, Contemporary Mathematics, §10.4 Polygons, Perimeter, and Circumference (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-contemporary-mathematics/blob/main/modules/m00128/index.cnxml): "Because this ratio is constant, our formula is accurate for any size circle." | [OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §25.7 The Formation of the Galaxy (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59955/index.cnxml): "Remember that the circumference of a circle is given by 2πR, where R is the radius of the circle." | OK |

Notes: c3 is derived from c1 (the formula's two sources): new radius - old radius = (C + 1)/(2π) - C/(2π) = 1/(2π) m = 0.159 m ≈ 16 cm. C cancels, so the same 16 cm holds for a basketball (C ≈ 0.75 m) or Earth. c2 derived: 2π × 6,371 km = 40,030 km ≈ 40,000 km. Assumes Earth is a perfect sphere and the rope is held in a circle at the same height all round. Quotes from the OpenStax source files on GitHub; '106' in the University Physics quote is 10^6 written without the superscript.
