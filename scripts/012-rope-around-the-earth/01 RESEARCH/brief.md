# Brief: add one meter to a rope around Earth

Series: Brain Breakers
Idea: A circle's radius is its length divided by 2π, so adding 1 m of rope always adds 1/(2π) m ≈ 16 cm of radius, whatever the circle's size: around Earth or around a basketball.
Misconception: Spread over a whole planet, one extra metre would lift the rope by a microscopic amount.
Hook angle: Wrap a rope around Earth, add one meter, and it rises sixteen centimeters all the way round.
Narrowed to: A perfect circle around a perfectly round Earth (equator); the rope is lifted evenly. No hills, no stretching.
Key condition to say aloud: Earth treated as a perfect sphere; the longer rope is held in a circle the same distance off the ground everywhere.
Candidate sources: OpenStax, Contemporary Mathematics, §10.4 Polygons, Perimeter, and Circumference (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-contemporary-mathematics/blob/main/modules/m00128/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §25.7 The Formation of the Galaxy (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59955/index.cnxml); OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §9.6 Center of Mass (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58323/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §21.5 Planets beyond the Solar System: Search and Discovery (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59921/index.cnxml)
Originality: A classic puzzle, usually told as a riddle with the answer. Ours puts the planet and a basketball side by side and makes the CANCELLING of Earth's size the visible moment.
Not used: Exact Earth circumference variations (equator vs poles); the 'rope pulled up into a tent at one point' version (a different and much harder answer).
