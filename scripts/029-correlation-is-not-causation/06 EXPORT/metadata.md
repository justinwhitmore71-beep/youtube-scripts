## Description
Umbrella sales and traffic jams can rise and fall together, but umbrellas don't cause traffic: rain drives both. Two things moving together is a correlation; a hidden third factor like the rain is called a confounding variable. To test a real cause, researchers assign it at random, which spreads hidden factors evenly. The umbrella example is invented to show the logic.

## Pinned comment (suggested)
Correlation isn't useless: it's often the first clue that something is going on. It just can't tell you WHICH way the cause runs, or whether a third factor is behind both. That's what randomized experiments are for.
