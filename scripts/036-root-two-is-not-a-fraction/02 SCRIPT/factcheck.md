# Fact-check: why no fraction equals the square root of 2 (both scripts)

| # | Claim | Source 1 | Source 2 | Status |

| c1 | An irrational number is one that cannot be written as a fraction of two integers; its decimal neither ends nor repeats. | [OpenStax, Contemporary Mathematics, §3.5 Irrational Numbers (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-contemporary-mathematics/blob/main/modules/m00045/index.cnxml): "Irrational numbers are numbers that cannot be expressed as a fraction of two integers." | [OpenStax, Algebra and Trigonometry 2e, §1.1 Real Numbers: Algebra Essentials (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-algebra-bundle/blob/main/modules/m51239/index.cnxml): "Irrational numbers cannot be expressed as a fraction of two integers." | OK |
| c2 | An odd number's square is odd, so if a² is even then a is even; then a = 2k and a² = 4k². | Proven on screen: (2k + 1)² = 4k² + 4k + 1 = 2(2k² + 2k) + 1, which is odd; so a² even forces a even, a = 2k, a² = 4k². From 2b² = 4k², b² = 2k², so b² is even and (same reason) b is even. | - | PROOF |
| c3 | √2 is irrational: if √2 = a/b in lowest terms, both a and b turn out even, a contradiction. | Proven on screen (the proof by contradiction in Levin's Discrete Mathematics, section 'Proofs'): √2 = a/b in lowest terms gives 2 = a²/b², so 2b² = a²; a² even ⇒ a even ⇒ a = 2k ⇒ 2b² = 4k² ⇒ b² = 2k² ⇒ b even; both even contradicts lowest terms. | - | PROOF |
| c4 | An irrational number's decimal never terminates or repeats; square roots of whole numbers that aren't perfect squares are irrational. | [OpenStax, Contemporary Mathematics, §3.5 Irrational Numbers (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-contemporary-mathematics/blob/main/modules/m00045/index.cnxml): "So irrational numbers must be those whose decimal representations do not terminate or become a repeating pattern. One collection of irrational numbers is square roots of numbers that aren’t perfect squares." | [Levin, Discrete Mathematics: An Open Introduction, Logic chapter, section 'Proofs' (University of Northern Colorado)](https://github.com/oscarlevin/discrete-book/blob/main/source/sec_logic-proofs.ptx): "Thus √2 is irrational." | OK |
| c5 | A perfect square has an even number of factors of 2, but twice a square has an odd number, so a² = 2b² is impossible for whole numbers. | Proven on screen: if a has m factors of 2, a² has 2m (even); if b has n, 2b² has 2n + 1 (odd). Prime factorizations are unique (c6), so a² and 2b² can't be the same number. | - | PROOF |
| c6 | Every whole number above 1 has exactly one prime factorization (apart from order). | [OpenStax, Contemporary Mathematics, §3.1 Prime and Composite Numbers (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-contemporary-mathematics/blob/main/modules/m00041/index.cnxml): "Every natural number, other than 1, can be expressed in exactly one way, apart from the arrangement, as a product of primes." | [Judson, Abstract Algebra: Theory and Applications, §2.2 The Division Algorithm: Prime Numbers (Stephen F. Austin State University)](https://github.com/twjudson/aata/blob/master/src/integers.xml): "Furthermore, this factorization is unique" | OK |
