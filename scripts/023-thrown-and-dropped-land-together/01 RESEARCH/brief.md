# Brief: why a thrown ball and a dropped ball land together

Series: Everyday Physics
Idea: Sideways and downward motion are independent: gravity pulls only down, and equally on both balls, so a ball thrown horizontally and a ball dropped from the same height fall the same ½gt² and hit level ground at the same instant.
Misconception: The thrown ball, moving faster, stays in the air longer (or the dropped one, 'falling straight', lands first).
Hook angle: Drop one ball and throw another sideways at the same moment: they land together.
Narrowed to: Horizontal throw from the same height, level ground, no air resistance. No angled throws, no range formula.
Key condition to say aloud: Thrown exactly sideways (horizontally) from the same height, level ground, air resistance ignored.
Candidate sources: OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §3.1 Kinematics in Two Dimensions: An Introduction (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42104/index.cnxml); OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §4.3 Projectile Motion (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58291/index.cnxml); OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §3.5 Free Fall (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58286/index.cnxml); OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §2.7 Falling Objects (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42102/index.cnxml)
Originality: Usually shown as a strobe photo with a caption. Ours makes the wrong guess the mascot's and puts the two fall clocks side by side; B uses the strobe frames as the proof.
Not used: Bullets fired vs dropped (popular, but needs air-resistance caveats); angled throws (do change the time, so they would muddy the idea).
