# Fact-check: why a thrown ball and a dropped ball land together (both scripts)

| # | Claim | Source 1 | Source 2 | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| c1 | Horizontal and vertical motion are independent: a ball thrown horizontally and a ball dropped from the same height are at the same height at every moment, so they land at the same time (level ground, no air resistance). | [OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §3.1 Kinematics in Two Dimensions: An Introduction (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42104/index.cnxml): "It is remarkable that for each flash of the strobe, the vertical positions of the two balls are the same. This similarity implies that the vertical motion is independent of whether or not the ball is moving horizontally." | [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §4.3 Projectile Motion (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58291/index.cnxml): "The most important fact to remember here is that motions along perpendicular axes are independent and thus can be analyzed separately." | OK |
| c2 | Ignoring air, the only force on a thrown or dropped ball is gravity, which acts straight down, so it gives no sideways push. | [OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §3.1 Kinematics in Two Dimensions: An Introduction (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42104/index.cnxml): "(Assuming no air resistance, the vertical motion of a falling object is influenced by gravity only, and not by any horizontal forces.)" | [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §4.3 Projectile Motion (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58291/index.cnxml): "Projectile motion is the motion of an object thrown or projected into the air, subject only to acceleration as a result of gravity." | OK |
| c3 | Starting with no downward speed, an object falls ½gt² in time t. | [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §3.5 Free Fall (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58286/index.cnxml): "y = y0 + v0t - 12gt2" | [OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §2.7 Falling Objects (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42102/index.cnxml): "y = y 0 + v 0 t - 1 2 gt 2" | OK |

Notes: c3: with v0 = 0 downward (true for both a dropped ball and one thrown exactly horizontally), y - y0 = -½gt², the same for both. Example for the animation (derived): from 1.0 m, t = √(2 × 1.0 / 9.8) = 0.45 s for both balls. Quotes from the OpenStax source files on GitHub; '12gt2' is ½gt² written without fractions or superscripts.
