# Fact-check: why Earth would be a 9 mm black hole (both scripts)

| # | Claim | Source 1 | Source 2 | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| c1 | A black hole forms when a mass is squeezed inside its Schwarzschild radius R = 2GM/c^2 (G the gravitational constant, M the mass, c the speed of light); inside it nothing, not even light, escapes, and the radius depends only on the mass. | [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §13.7 Einstein's Theory of Gravity (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58352/index.cnxml): "For any mass M, if that mass were compressed to the extent that its radius becomes less than the Schwarzschild radius, then the mass will collapse to a singularity, and anything that passes inside that radius cannot escape." | [OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §24.6 Black Holes (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59943/index.cnxml): "where c is the speed of light, G is the gravitational constant, and M is the mass of the black hole." | OK |
| c2 | For Earth's mass, that radius is about 9 mm (8.85 mm), a ball under 2 cm across, less than the size of a grape. | [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §13.7 Einstein's Theory of Gravity (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58352/index.cnxml): "If we use the mass of Earth, we get RS = 8.85 × 10^-3m. This is a diameter of less than 2 cm!" | [OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §24.6 Black Holes (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59943/index.cnxml): "for Earth to become a black hole, it would have to be compressed to a radius of only 1 centimeter—less than the size of a grape." | OK |
| c3 | The radius is small because c^2 (the speed of light squared) is a huge number in the denominator while G is small. | [OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §34.2 General Relativity and Quantum Gravity (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42689/index.cnxml): "Since G is small and c2 is large, you can see that black holes are extremely small, only a few kilometers for masses a little greater than the Sun’s." | [OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §24.6 Black Holes (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59943/index.cnxml): "Note that in this formula, 2, G, and c are all constant; only the mass changes from black hole to black hole." | OK |
| c4 | For the Sun's mass, the radius is about 3 km. | [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §13.7 Einstein's Theory of Gravity (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58352/index.cnxml): "= 2.95 × 103m. This is a diameter of only about 6 km." | [OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §24.6 Black Holes (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59943/index.cnxml): "the Schwarzschild radius would be about 3 kilometers" | OK |

Notes: Arithmetic (University Physics Vol. 1 §13.7, with G = 6.67 × 10^-11 N·m²/kg², M_Earth = 5.97 × 10^24 kg, c = 3.0 × 10^8 m/s): R = 2(6.67e-11)(5.97e24)/(3.0e8)² = 8.85e-3 m ≈ 9 mm. 'About nine millimeters' rounds 8.85 mm; Astronomy 2e rounds the same radius to 1 cm. c3: source 2 supports that only M varies while 2, G and c are fixed constants; source 1 states the 'small G, large c²' reason directly. Thought-experiment condition: University Physics §13.7 also says 'There is no known mechanism that could cause an Earth-sized object to become a black hole.' (one source; used in the pinned comment only). Quotes from the OpenStax source files on GitHub; superscripts appear as plain text (c2 = c², 103 = 10³).
