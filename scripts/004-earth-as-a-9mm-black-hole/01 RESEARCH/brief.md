# Brief: why Earth would be a 9 mm black hole

Series: The Universe, Explained
Idea: A black hole's radius depends only on its mass, R = 2GM/c^2, and because c^2 is enormous, the radius for Earth's mass is only about 9 mm.
Misconception: Black holes have to be huge; only giant things can become one.
Hook angle: Crush the whole Earth into a ball smaller than a grape and it becomes a black hole.
Narrowed to: Non-spinning black hole, Schwarzschild radius only. No singularities, spaghettification or Hawking radiation.
Key condition to say aloud: A thought experiment: nothing known could actually compress Earth that far.
Candidate sources: OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §13.7 Einstein's Theory of Gravity (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58352/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §24.5 Black Holes (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59943/index.cnxml); OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §34.2 General Relativity and Quantum Gravity (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42689/index.cnxml)
Originality: Usually told as a size fact ('Earth would be the size of a marble'). Ours builds R = 2GM/c^2 and shows WHY it is tiny: c^2 is a huge number on the bottom.
Not used: Density comparison with neutron stars (true, one source here); 'Earth will never become a black hole' wording beyond the UP quote; marble comparisons (sources say grape / under 2 cm).
