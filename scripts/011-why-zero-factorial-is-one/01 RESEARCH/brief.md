# Brief: why 0! = 1

Series: Simple Proofs
Idea: Each factorial is its number times the one below (n! = n × (n−1)!), so stepping down the staircase means dividing: 24, 6, 2, 1, and one more step gives 0! = 1/1 = 1.
Misconception: 0! should be 0, because multiplying 'nothing' or anything with a zero gives zero.
Hook angle: Zero factorial is one, not zero, and a staircase proves it.
Narrowed to: Whole numbers only. No gamma function, no empty-product formalism beyond the staircase (A) and the permutation formula (B).
Key condition to say aloud: Factorials of whole numbers; 0! is the natural continuation of the pattern (textbooks define it as 1).
Candidate sources: OpenStax, Algebra and Trigonometry 2e, §13.1 Sequences and Their Notations (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-algebra-bundle/blob/main/modules/m49444/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Contemporary Mathematics, §7.2 Permutations (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-contemporary-mathematics/blob/main/modules/m00086/index.cnxml); Levin, Discrete Mathematics: An Open Introduction, Discrete Structures chapter, section 'Functions' (University of Northern Colorado) (https://github.com/oscarlevin/discrete-book/blob/main/source/sec_structures-functions.ptx); OpenStax, Calculus Volume 2, §5.6 Ratio and Root Tests (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-calculus-bundle/blob/main/modules/m53747/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Algebra and Trigonometry 2e, §13.5 Counting Principles (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-algebra-bundle/blob/main/modules/m49448/index.cnxml)
Originality: Often answered with 'it's a convention' or the empty product. Ours walks DOWN the staircase dividing, so 1 is forced; B shows the counting formula would break otherwise.
Not used: 'There is exactly one way to arrange nothing' (true, but not found in two sources this session); the gamma function.
