# Brief: why moving clocks tick slower (the light clock)

Series: One Equation
Idea: A clock made of light bouncing between two mirrors, watched as it moves past you, sends its light along a longer slanted path; since light's speed is the same for everyone, each tick must take longer, and Pythagoras turns the triangle into Δt = Δτ / √(1 − v²/c²).
Misconception: Time is the same for everyone; moving can't change how fast a clock ticks.
Hook angle: A clock moving past you really ticks slower, and a bouncing light beam shows why.
Narrowed to: Special relativity only, one light clock, one observer watching it pass. No twin paradox, no gravity (GPS), no length contraction.
Key condition to say aloud: The slowing is seen by someone the clock moves past; at everyday speeds it is far too small to notice.
Candidate sources: OpenStax, University Physics Volume 3, §5.3 Time Dilation (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58563/index.cnxml); OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §28.2 Simultaneity And Time Dilation (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42531/index.cnxml)
Originality: Usually shown with a spaceship and a long derivation. Ours keeps just the zigzag and one sentence of 'why' (same speed, longer path), then lets the triangle build the equation.
Not used: Muon lifetimes (strong evidence, two sources, but a second topic); GPS clock drift (only one source found for the numbers in this session); 'clocks stop at light speed' (true limit, but invites misreading).
