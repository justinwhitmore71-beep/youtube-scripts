## Description
Make a clock from light bouncing between two mirrors. When it moves past you, you see its light zigzag along a longer slanted path, and because light's speed is the same for everyone, each tick takes longer. Pythagoras turns that triangle into Δt = Δτ/√(1 − v²/c²). At everyday speeds the effect is far too small to notice.

## Pinned comment (suggested)
It's real but tiny at everyday speeds: at highway speed (30 m/s) a tick stretches by about 5 parts in a million billion. It shows up clearly for particles moving near light speed, such as muons made by cosmic rays, which live longer than they should.
