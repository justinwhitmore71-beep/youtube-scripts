| # | Claim | Source 1 | Source 2 | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| c1 | Sunlight takes a little over 8 minutes (about 500 seconds) to reach Earth, so we see the Sun as it was about 8 minutes ago. | [OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §1.6 A Tour of the Universe (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59753/index.cnxml): "Light takes slightly more than 8 minutes to travel 1 astronomical unit, which means the latest news we receive from the Sun is always 8 minutes old." | [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §1.1 The Scope and Scale of Physics (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58269/index.cnxml): "(g) The mean Earth–Sun distance: 1.5 × 10^11m" | OK |
| c2 | Light travels about 300,000 km every second (3.00 × 10^8 m/s). | [OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, ch. 5 Radiation and Spectra, chapter opener (Thinking Ahead) (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59791/index.cnxml): "Even light, which travels at a speed of 300,000 kilometers per second (km/s), takes more than 4 years to reach us from the nearest star." | [OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §24.3 The Electromagnetic Spectrum (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42444/index.cnxml): "where c=3.00×108m/s is the speed of light" | OK |
| c3 | The Sun is about 150 million km from Earth on average. | [OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §1.6 A Tour of the Universe (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59753/index.cnxml): "Earth revolves around our star, the Sun, which is about 150 million kilometers away" | [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §1.1 The Scope and Scale of Physics (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58269/index.cnxml): "(g) The mean Earth–Sun distance: 1.5 × 10^11m" | OK |
| c4 | Light from distant objects is older still: looking at distant galaxies means seeing them as they were millions or billions of years ago. | [OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §28.1 Observations of Distant Galaxies (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59970/index.cnxml): "When we look at distant galaxies, we are looking back in time." | [OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §34.1 Cosmology and Particle Physics (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42686/index.cnxml): "For example, the Andromeda galaxy is 2 million light years away, so that the light now reaching us left it 2 million years ago." | OK |

Notes: c1 source 2 gives the distance; the time is derived: 1.5 × 10^11 m ÷ 3.00 × 10^8 m/s = 500 s = 8 min 20 s, matching source 1's 'slightly more than 8 minutes'. 1.5 × 10^11 m = 150 million km. A's 'If the Sun switched off, you'd still see it shining for eight more minutes' follows from c1 (light already on its way keeps arriving). Quotes from the OpenStax source files on GitHub ('108' is 10^8 written without a superscript).
