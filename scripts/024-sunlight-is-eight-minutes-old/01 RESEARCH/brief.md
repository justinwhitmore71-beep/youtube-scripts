# Brief: why the Sun you see is eight minutes old

Series: The Universe, Explained
Idea: Light is fast (300,000 km/s) but not instant, and the Sun is about 150 million km away, so its light takes distance ÷ speed ≈ 500 s, a bit over 8 minutes, to reach us: we always see the Sun as it was.
Misconception: Light arrives instantly, so we see the Sun as it is right now.
Hook angle: The Sun you see right now is eight minutes old.
Narrowed to: Sun to Earth only, plus one line on distant galaxies. No relativity, no 'what if the Sun vanished' gravity debate.
Key condition to say aloud: Average Earth-Sun distance (it varies a little through the year).
Candidate sources: OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §1.6 A Tour of the Universe (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59753/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, ch. 5 Radiation and Spectra, chapter opener (Thinking Ahead) (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59791/index.cnxml); OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §24.3 The Electromagnetic Spectrum (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42444/index.cnxml); OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §1.1 The Scope and Scale of Physics (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58269/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §28.1 Observations of Distant Galaxies (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59970/index.cnxml); OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §34.1 Cosmology and Particle Physics (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42686/index.cnxml)
Originality: Usually stated as a fact. Ours builds t = d / c with the two round numbers, so viewers can do the division themselves, then widens it to 'looking out is looking back'.
Not used: 'If the Sun vanished, Earth would keep orbiting for 8 minutes' (true in general relativity, but no source opened here); the nearest star's 4-year light (one source).
