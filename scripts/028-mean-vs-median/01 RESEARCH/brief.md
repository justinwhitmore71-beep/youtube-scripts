# Brief: why one rich person wrecks the average but not the median

Series: Brain Breakers
Idea: The mean adds every value and shares it out, so one extreme value drags it far; the median just takes the middle of the line-up, so it barely moves. With nine salaries of $40,000 and one of $10 million, the mean is $1,036,000 and the median $40,000.
Misconception: The average is the typical value.
Hook angle: One rich person can push a room's average salary past a million dollars.
Narrowed to: Mean vs median with one invented example of ten salaries. No skewness measures, no modes.
Key condition to say aloud: The effect is big only when a few values are extreme compared with the rest.
Candidate sources: OpenIntro Statistics (Diez, Çetinkaya-Rundel & Barr), ch. 2 Summarizing data (https://github.com/OpenIntroStat/openintro-statistics/blob/master/ch_summarizing_data/TeX/ch_summarizing_data.tex); OpenStax, Introductory Statistics 2e, §2.5 Measures of the Center of the Data (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-introductory-statistics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m46928/index.cnxml); OpenIntro, Introduction to Modern Statistics (Çetinkaya-Rundel & Hardin), Exploring numerical data: Robust statistics (https://github.com/OpenIntroStat/ims/blob/main/explore-numerical.qmd)
Originality: Often told as a celebrity-walks-into-a-bar joke. Ours lines the ten people up on screen, so the median is literally the person in the middle while the mean bar shoots past all of them.
Not used: Real income statistics (would need sources and dates); skewed distributions in general (one more idea).
