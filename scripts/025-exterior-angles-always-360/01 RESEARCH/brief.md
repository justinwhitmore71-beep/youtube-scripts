# Brief: why a polygon's outside angles always add to 360°

Series: Simple Proofs
Idea: Walk around a polygon and turn at each corner: the turns are the exterior angles, and when you get back you face the way you started, so together they make exactly one full turn, 360°, whatever the number of sides.
Misconception: Shapes with more corners must have more total 'outside angle'.
Hook angle: Walk around any dent-free shape with straight sides, and your turns add up to three hundred sixty degrees.
Narrowed to: Convex (dent-free) polygons, walked one way round. No interior-angle formula on screen.
Key condition to say aloud: The shape has no dents (convex); for shapes with dents it still works if turns the other way count as negative (pinned comment).
Candidate sources: OpenStax, Algebra and Trigonometry 2e, §7.1 Angles (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-algebra-bundle/blob/main/modules/m51283/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Calculus Volume 1, §1.3 Trigonometric Functions (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-calculus-bundle/blob/main/modules/m53479/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Contemporary Mathematics, §10.4 Polygons, Perimeter, and Circumference (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-contemporary-mathematics/blob/main/modules/m00128/index.cnxml)
Originality: Textbooks give 360°/n for regular polygons and a formula. Ours makes the viewer the walker: the sum is 360° because you end facing the same way. Works for lopsided shapes, not just regular ones.
Not used: The interior-angle formula (n − 2) × 180° (only one source opened here); the turtle-graphics version; star polygons.
