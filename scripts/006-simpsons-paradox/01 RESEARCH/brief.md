# Brief: Simpson's paradox: better at both, worse overall

Series: Brain Breakers
Idea: A player can have the better success rate in every category and the worse rate overall, because the overall rate also depends on how each player's attempts are split between easy and hard categories.
Misconception: If someone is better in every group, they must be better overall.
Hook angle: A player can beat a rival at close shots and at long shots, and still lose overall.
Narrowed to: One invented two-player, two-shot-type example with round numbers; no real dataset, no regression.
Key condition to say aloud: The two players take very different mixes of easy and hard shots (the imbalanced third variable).
Candidate sources: OpenIntro, Introduction to Modern Statistics (Çetinkaya-Rundel & Hardin), Applications: Explore, Simpson's paradox (https://github.com/OpenIntroStat/ims/blob/main/data-applications.qmd); Poldrack, Statistical Thinking for the 21st Century, ch. 12 Categorical relationships: Beware of Simpson's paradox (Stanford University) (https://github.com/statsthinking21/statsthinking21-core/blob/master/12-CategoricalRelationships.Rmd)
Originality: Textbooks use real datasets (batting averages, race times, admissions). Ours uses invented round numbers so the arithmetic fits on screen, and makes the shot MIX the visible culprit.
Not used: The Jeter/Justice batting averages and the Berkeley admissions case (real, but each found in only one source here); kidney-stone treatment data (not sourced in this session).
