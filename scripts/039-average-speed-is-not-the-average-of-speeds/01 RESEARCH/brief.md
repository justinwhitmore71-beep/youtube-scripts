# Brief: why 30 there and 90 back averages 45, not 60

Series: Brain Breakers
Idea: Average speed is total distance ÷ total time. Driving 60 km at 30 km/h takes 2 hours and coming back at 90 km/h takes 40 minutes, so 120 km in 2⅔ hours is 45 km/h: you spend more TIME at the slow speed, so it counts for more.
Misconception: The average of 30 and 90 is 60, so the average speed is 60 km/h.
Hook angle: Drive there at thirty and back at ninety, and you average forty-five, not sixty.
Narrowed to: One invented round trip with equal distances. No harmonic-mean formula on screen, no velocity vs speed.
Key condition to say aloud: Equal distances each way; average speed means total distance over total time.
Candidate sources: OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §1.2 Physical Quantities and Units (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42091/index.cnxml); OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §1.2 Units and Standards (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58270/index.cnxml)
Originality: A classic puzzle, often stated as 'can you average 60 if you went there at 30?' Ours puts the two legs on a timeline so the viewer SEES the slow leg taking three times as long.
Not used: The harmonic mean formula (correct, but a second idea); 'you can never average 60 if the first half was at 30' variants.
