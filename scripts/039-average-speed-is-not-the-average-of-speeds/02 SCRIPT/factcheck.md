| c1 | Average speed is the total distance traveled divided by the total time taken. | [OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §1.2 Physical Quantities and Units (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42091/index.cnxml): "Average speed is distance traveled divided by time of travel." | [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §1.2 Units and Standards (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58270/index.cnxml): "we could define average speed by stating that it is calculated as the total distance traveled divided by time of travel." | OK |
| c2 | Invented trip: 60 km at 30 km/h takes 2 h; 60 km at 90 km/h takes 40 min; 120 km in 2 h 40 min (2⅔ h) is 45 km/h. Time at 30 is three times the time at 90, so the time-weighted average is (3 × 30 + 1 × 90) / 4 = 45. | Arithmetic shown on screen: 60/30 = 2 h; 60/90 = 2/3 h = 40 min; total 2⅔ h = 8/3 h; 120 ÷ 8/3 = 45 km/h. Check: (3 × 30 + 90)/4 = 180/4 = 45. | - | PROOF |

Notes: c2 is arithmetic on an invented trip (nothing to source; the playbook reserves PROOF for Simple Proofs, so Justin to confirm). The answer doesn't depend on the 60 km: for any distance d, total time is d/30 + d/90 = 4d/90, so the average is 2d ÷ (4d/90) = 45 km/h.
