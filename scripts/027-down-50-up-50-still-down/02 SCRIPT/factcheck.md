# Fact-check: why down 50% then up 50% leaves you down 25% (both scripts)

| c1 | A percent increase or decrease is measured as a percent of the original (starting) amount. | [OpenStax, Prealgebra 2e, §6.2 Solve General Applications of Percent (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-prealgebra-bundle/blob/main/modules/m81313/index.cnxml): "Then we find what percent the amount of increase is of the original amount." | [OpenStax, Contemporary Mathematics, §6.2 Discounts, Markups, and Sales Tax (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-contemporary-mathematics/blob/main/modules/m00058/index.cnxml): "In a discount application, the discount plays the role of the part, the percent discount is the percentage, and the original price plays the role of the total." | OK |
| c2 | A percent means 'per hundred': a part of a total, part = percentage × total. | [OpenStax, Prealgebra 2e, §6.1 Understand Percent (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-prealgebra-bundle/blob/main/modules/m81306/index.cnxml): "A percent is a ratio whose denominator is 100 ." | [OpenStax, Contemporary Mathematics, §6.2 Discounts, Markups, and Sales Tax (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-contemporary-mathematics/blob/main/modules/m00058/index.cnxml): "The formula used was part=percentage×total." | OK |
| c3 | $100 down 50% is $50; $50 up 50% is $75. In the other order, $100 up 50% is $150, and down 50% is $75. Either way the factors multiply: 0.5 × 1.5 = 1.5 × 0.5 = 0.75, a 25% loss. | Arithmetic shown on screen: 50% of 100 = 50, 100 − 50 = 50; 50% of 50 = 25, 50 + 25 = 75. 100 + 50% of 100 = 150; 150 − 50% of 150 = 150 − 75 = 75. 100 × 0.5 × 1.5 = 100 × 1.5 × 0.5 = 75. | - | PROOF |
| c4 | After a 50% loss, getting back to the start needs a 100% gain. | Arithmetic shown on screen: from $50, reaching $100 needs +$50, and 50 is 100% of 50. | - | PROOF |

Notes: c1 and c2 are definitions (two OpenStax books: Prealgebra 2e and Contemporary Mathematics, different author teams); c3 and c4 are arithmetic proven on screen.
