the wrong guess is 'twice as far'

1[HOOK] Drive twice as fast, and braking takes four times the distance.

2[MASCOT: Shouldn't it be twice?] Twice the speed, twice the distance, surely?

3[EQUATION: KE = ½ m v²] A moving car's energy is half its mass times its speed squared.

4 Squared means double the speed gives four times the energy.

5 The same brakes on the same road remove that energy at a steady rate per meter.

6 So four times the energy takes four times the meters to stop.

7[TAKEAWAY] Stopping distance grows with speed squared, so a little extra speed costs a lot.

1[HOOK] At thirty meters per second, a car on dry concrete brakes in sixty-four meters.

2[MASCOT: Half the speed, half the distance?] So at half that speed, thirty-two meters?

3 No: at fifteen meters per second it needs only about sixteen.

4[EQUATION: d = v² / 2a] Braking distance is speed squared, over twice the braking rate.

5 The car's energy, one half m v squared, also grows with speed squared.

6 Half the speed leaves a quarter of the energy for the brakes to absorb.

7[TAKEAWAY] That's why a few extra kilometers per hour add many meters to every stop.
