# Brief: why twice the speed needs four times the braking distance

Series: One Equation
Idea: A moving car's kinetic energy is ½mv², so doubling the speed quadruples the energy; the brakes remove energy at a steady rate per metre (force × distance), so the braking distance also quadruples.
Misconception: Twice as fast means twice as far to stop.
Hook angle: Drive twice as fast, and braking takes four times the distance.
Narrowed to: Braking distance only (after the brakes are on), same car, same brakes, same road, constant braking force. Reaction distance (which only doubles) left out.
Key condition to say aloud: Same brakes on the same road, so the braking force is the same; reaction time not included.
Candidate sources: OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §7.2 Kinetic Energy and the Work-Energy Theorem (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42147/index.cnxml); OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §7.2 Kinetic Energy (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58308/index.cnxml); OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §7.3 Work-Energy Theorem (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58309/index.cnxml); OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §2.5 Motion Equations for Constant Acceleration in One Dimension (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42099/index.cnxml); OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §3.4 Motion with Constant Acceleration (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58285/index.cnxml)
Originality: Usually given as a stopping-distance table. Ours builds KE = ½mv² symbol by symbol and makes 'squared' the reason; B uses the textbook's own 30 m/s → 64 m example and halves it.
Not used: Reaction distance (it grows only in proportion to speed); wet-road numbers (90 m from 30 m/s, two sources, could be a pinned comment); crash energy.
