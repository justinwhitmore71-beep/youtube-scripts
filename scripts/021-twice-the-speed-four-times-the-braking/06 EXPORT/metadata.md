5. The 'squared' that makes speeding dangerous

## Description
A moving car's energy is ½mv², so doubling the speed gives four times the energy. The same brakes on the same road remove energy at a steady rate per metre, so the braking distance also goes up four times. A car braking at 7 m/s² from 30 m/s stops in about 64 m; from 15 m/s, about 16 m.

## Pinned comment (suggested)
This is braking distance only. Your total stopping distance also includes the distance you travel before your foot hits the brake, and that part only doubles when speed doubles, so the total grows a bit less than 4×. But it still grows much faster than speed.
