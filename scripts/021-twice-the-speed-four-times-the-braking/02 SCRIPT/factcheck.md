# Fact-check: why twice the speed needs four times the braking distance (both scripts)

| # | Claim | Source 1 | Source 2 | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| c1 | A moving object's (kinetic) energy is ½mv²: half the mass times the speed squared. | [OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §7.2 Kinetic Energy and the Work-Energy Theorem (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42147/index.cnxml): "In equation form, the translational kinetic energy, KE = 1 2 mv 2 , is the energy associated with translational motion." | [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §7.2 Kinetic Energy (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58308/index.cnxml): "The kinetic energy of a particle is one-half the product of the particle’s mass m and the square of its speed v" | OK |
| c2 | The net work done on the car (here the braking force times the braking distance) equals its change in kinetic energy, so with the same braking force the distance is proportional to the energy removed. | [OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §7.2 Kinetic Energy and the Work-Energy Theorem (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42147/index.cnxml): "The net work on a system equals the change in the quantity 12mv2." | [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §7.3 Work-Energy Theorem (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58309/index.cnxml): "The net work done on a particle equals the change in the particle’s kinetic energy" | OK |
| c3 | Braking distance = v²/(2a): a car braking from 30.0 m/s at 7.00 m/s² (dry concrete) stops in 64.3 m. | [OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §2.5 Motion Equations for Constant Acceleration in One Dimension (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42099/index.cnxml): "x - 0 = 0 2 - 30 . 0 m/s^2 2 - 7 . 00 m/s 2 Thus, x = 64 . 3 m on dry concrete ." | [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §3.4 Motion with Constant Acceleration (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58285/index.cnxml): "Thus, x = 64.3m on dry concrete." | OK |
| c4 | Doubling the speed (same braking) gives four times the braking distance: from 15 m/s the same car needs about 16 m, a quarter of 64.3 m. | [OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §2.5 Motion Equations for Constant Acceleration in One Dimension (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42099/index.cnxml): "x - x 0 = v 2 - v 0 2 2a" | [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 1, §3.4 Motion with Constant Acceleration (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58285/index.cnxml): "we get v2 = v02 + 2a(x - x0)(constanta)" | OK |

Notes: c4 derived from the braking formula (c3): d = v²/(2a) with a = 7.00 m/s²: 30²/14 = 64.3 m; 15²/14 = 16.1 m; ratio (30/15)² = 4. Energy view (c1, c2): with the same braking force f, f × d = ½mv², so d ∝ v². 30 m/s = 108 km/h. Both textbooks use the same dry/wet concrete example (OpenStax, two different books). Quotes from the OpenStax source files on GitHub; superscripts appear as plain text (mv 2 = mv², 12mv2 = ½mv²).
