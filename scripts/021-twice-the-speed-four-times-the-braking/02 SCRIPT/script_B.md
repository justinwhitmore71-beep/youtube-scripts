[HOOK] At thirty meters per second, a car on dry concrete brakes in sixty-four meters. {c3}
[MASCOT: Half the speed, half the distance?] So at half that speed, thirty-two meters?
No: at fifteen meters per second it needs only about sixteen. {c4}
[EQUATION: d = v² / 2a] Braking distance is speed squared, over twice the braking rate. {c3} {c4}
The car's energy, one half m v squared, also grows with speed squared. {c1}
Half the speed leaves a quarter of the energy for the brakes to absorb. {c1} {c2}
[TAKEAWAY] That's why a few extra kilometers per hour add many meters to every stop. {c4}
