# Brief: why tails is never 'due'

Series: Brain Breakers
Idea: Coin flips are independent, so after five heads the next flip is still 1/2; six heads in a row and five heads then a tail are equally likely (1/64 each), because they differ only in that last fifty-fifty flip.
Misconception: After a run of heads, tails is 'due' to balance things out (the gambler's fallacy).
Hook angle: After five heads in a row, the next flip is still exactly fifty-fifty.
Narrowed to: A fair coin, six flips. No roulette house edge, no law of large numbers.
Key condition to say aloud: A fair coin (each side 1/2) and fair, independent flips.
Candidate sources: OpenIntro Statistics (Diez, Çetinkaya-Rundel & Barr), ch. 3 Probability (https://github.com/OpenIntroStat/openintro-statistics/blob/master/ch_probability/TeX/ch_probability.tex); Poldrack, Statistical Thinking for the 21st Century, ch. 8 Resampling and simulation: What is randomness? (Stanford University) (https://github.com/statsthinking21/statsthinking21-core/blob/master/08-Resampling.Rmd); OpenStax, Contemporary Mathematics, §7.4 Tree Diagrams, Tables, and Outcomes (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-contemporary-mathematics/blob/main/modules/m00088/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Introductory Statistics 2e, §3.2 Independent and Mutually Exclusive Events (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-introductory-statistics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m46948/index.cnxml)
Originality: Usually told as 'the coin has no memory' and left there. Ours puts the two six-flip sequences side by side (B) and shows they share the first five flips, so the whole difference is one fifty-fifty flip.
Not used: Casinos posting recent results to tempt gamblers (OpenIntro says this; only one source); 'it evens out in the long run' (true in proportion, but easy to misstate; a separate Short).
