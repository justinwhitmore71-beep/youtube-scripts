# Brief: why astronauts float in almost full gravity

Series: The Universe, Explained
Idea: At the space station's height (about 400 km) gravity is still about 8.7 m/s², nearly 90% of the 9.8 at the ground, because 400 km barely changes the distance from Earth's centre; astronauts float because they and the station are falling together, moving sideways so fast that Earth curves away beneath them.
Misconception: There is no gravity in space; that's why astronauts float.
Hook angle: Gravity on the space station is almost ninety percent as strong as on the ground.
Narrowed to: Low Earth orbit, the ISS only. No microgravity science, no artificial gravity.
Key condition to say aloud: Gravity's pull on the astronauts is about 90% of what it is on the ground; what they feel is weightlessness, because a scale falling with them reads zero.
