1[HOOK] A bet that pays four to one on a die roll still loses you money.

2[MASCOT: Four to one sounds great!] Four dollars for one sounds great, right?

3 Roll a six and win four dollars; roll anything else and lose one.

4[EQUATION: EV = 4 × 1/6 − 1 × 5/6 = −1/6] Expected value weighs each result by its chance: four sixths minus five sixths.

5 That's minus one sixth: about seventeen cents lost every roll, on average.

6 You win big but rarely, and lose small five times as often.

7[TAKEAWAY] Judge a bet by expected value, not payout: the long run always collects.

The fair price is five

find the payout that breaks even
85 words, about 38 s

1[HOOK] On a die roll, four-to-one on a six loses money; five-to-one would be fair.

2[MASCOT: Why five?] Why exactly five?

3[EQUATION: EV = 5 × 1/6 − 1 × 5/6 = 0] Expected value adds each result times its chance.

4 A six comes once in six rolls; the other five rolls each cost you a dollar.

5 To break even, that one win must pay back all five losses: five dollars.

6 Pay only four, and you lose a dollar every six rolls on average.

7[TAKEAWAY] Fair odds match the payout to the chances; anything less quietly drains you.
