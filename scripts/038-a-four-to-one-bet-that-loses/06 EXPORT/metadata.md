## Description
Roll a die: a six wins you $4, anything else loses $1. It sounds generous, but the expected value, each result times its chance, is 4 × 1/6 − 1 × 5/6 = −1/6, a loss of about 17 cents per roll on average. You win big but rarely and lose small but five times as often. A $5 payout would be exactly fair. The bet is an invented example.

## Pinned comment (suggested)
'On average' matters: you can win this bet several times in a row. Expected value says what happens over many rolls, and over many rolls a negative expected value always shows up as a loss. That's the maths behind games built to favour the house.
