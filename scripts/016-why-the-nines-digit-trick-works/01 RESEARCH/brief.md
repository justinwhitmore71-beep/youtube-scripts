# Brief: why adding the digits tests for 9

Series: Simple Proofs
Idea: Ten is 9 + 1, a hundred is 99 + 1, and so on, so any number is a pile of nines plus the sum of its digits; the pile always divides by 9, so the digit sum alone decides.
Misconception: The digit-sum test is a coincidence or a memorised trick with no reason behind it.
Hook angle: Add up a number's digits, and you instantly know if nine divides it.
Narrowed to: Divisibility by 9 for whole numbers, one 3-digit example (A) or 4-digit example (B). Mentions 3 only in the notes.
Key condition to say aloud: Whole numbers written in base ten.
Candidate sources: OpenStax, Contemporary Mathematics, §3.1 Prime and Composite Numbers (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-contemporary-mathematics/blob/main/modules/m00041/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Prealgebra 2e, §2.4 Find Multiples and Factors (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-prealgebra-bundle/blob/main/modules/m81272/index.cnxml)
Originality: Textbooks list the rule in a table. Ours shows WHY: each hundred and ten is split into 'nines plus one', so the leftover ones are exactly the digits.
Not used: The rule for 3 (same reason, two sources: Contemporary Mathematics §3.1 and Prealgebra 2e §2.4); casting out nines to check sums; the scrambled-digits trick.
