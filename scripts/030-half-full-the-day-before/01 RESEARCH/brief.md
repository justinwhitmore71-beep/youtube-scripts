# Brief: why a doubling pond is only half covered the day before

Series: Brain Breakers
Idea: If something doubles every day and is complete on day 30, then running time backwards halves it each day: half on day 29, a quarter on day 28, about 3% on day 25. Doubling growth looks like almost nothing until the very end.
Misconception: If it's full on day 30, it was about half full around day 15.
Hook angle: Algae doubling daily covers a pond on day thirty; on day twenty-nine it covered only half.
Narrowed to: One invented doubling example (algae on a pond), counted backwards. No logarithms, no real epidemics or finance.
Key condition to say aloud: Steady doubling every day (a fixed doubling time).
Candidate sources: OpenStax, Algebra and Trigonometry 2e, §6.1 Exponential Functions (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-algebra-bundle/blob/main/modules/m49361/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Calculus Volume 1, §6.8 Exponential Growth and Decay (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-calculus-bundle/blob/main/modules/m53651/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Algebra and Trigonometry 2e, §6.7 Exponential and Logarithmic Models (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-algebra-bundle/blob/main/modules/m49367/index.cnxml)
Originality: A classic riddle. Ours counts BACKWARDS from the full pond, so the halving is the visual, and B contrasts adding a fixed amount with doubling.
Not used: Real-world doubling claims (viruses, debt, the chessboard and rice: would need sources); doubling-time formulas with ln 2.
