# Fact-check: why a circle's area is πr² (both scripts)

| # | Claim | Source 1 | Source 2 | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| c1 | The area of a circle of radius r is πr². | [OpenStax, Contemporary Mathematics, §10.6 Area (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-contemporary-mathematics/blob/main/modules/m00130/index.cnxml): "The area of a circle is given as A=πr2, where r is the radius." | [OpenStax, Calculus Volume 1, §1.1 Review of Functions (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-calculus-bundle/blob/main/modules/m53477/index.cnxml): "For example, the area of a circle of radius r is given by the formula A ( r ) = πr2 ." | OK |
| c2 | The circumference (the 'crust') of a circle is 2πr. | [OpenStax, Contemporary Mathematics, §10.4 Polygons, Perimeter, and Circumference (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-contemporary-mathematics/blob/main/modules/m00128/index.cnxml): "C=2πr, where r is the radius." | [OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §25.6 The Formation of the Galaxy (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59955/index.cnxml): "Remember that the circumference of a circle is given by 2πR, where R is the radius of the circle." | OK |
| c3 | Thin wedges laid tip-up and tip-down approach a rectangle of height r and width half the circumference, πr, so the area is r × πr = πr². | Shown on screen: n equal wedges, alternated, form a shape whose top and bottom edges are each half the circumference (n/2 arcs = πr) and whose slanted sides have length r; as n grows the arcs flatten and the sides become vertical, so the area tends to r × πr = πr². | - | PROOF |
| c4 | A triangle's area is half its base times its height. | [OpenStax, Contemporary Mathematics, §10.6 Area (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-contemporary-mathematics/blob/main/modules/m00130/index.cnxml): "The area of a triangle is given as A=12bh, where b represents the base and h represents the height." | [OpenStax, Prealgebra 2e, §9.4 Use Properties of Rectangles, Triangles, and Trapezoids (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-prealgebra-bundle/blob/main/modules/m81332/index.cnxml): "The formula for the area of a triangle is A = 12bh , where b is the base" | OK |
| c5 | Thin rings, straightened, stack into a triangle with base 2πr (the outer circumference) and height r, so the area is ½ × 2πr × r = πr². | Shown on screen: a ring at distance s from the centre has length 2πs, which grows evenly from 0 at the centre to 2πr at the edge; stacked, the straightened rings fill a triangle of base 2πr and height r; ½ × 2πr × r = πr². | - | PROOF |
| c6 | Doubling the radius multiplies the area by four: π(2r)² = 4πr²; in the pictures, both the rectangle's height and width (or the triangle's base and height) double. | Proven on screen: π(2r)² = π × 4r² = 4πr²; rectangle 2r tall and 2πr wide has area 4πr². | - | PROOF |

Notes: c3 and c5 are visual limit arguments: the shapes become exact only as the slices or rings get thinner, which the animation should show. In the sources, '12bh' is ½bh and 'πr2' is πr² written without fractions or superscripts.
