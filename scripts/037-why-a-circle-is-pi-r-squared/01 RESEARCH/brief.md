# Brief: why a circle's area is πr²

Series: Simple Proofs
Idea: Cut a circle into thin wedges and line them up, tips alternating: the shape approaches a rectangle with height r and width half the circumference (πr), so the area is πr × r = πr².
Misconception: A circle twice as wide has twice the area (and A = πr² is just a formula to memorise).
Hook angle: A pizza twice as wide has four times as much pizza.
Narrowed to: The rearrangement picture (A) and the unrolled-rings picture (B). No integrals on screen.
Key condition to say aloud: The rectangle is exact only in the limit of thinner and thinner slices; any finite number of slices gives a wavy edge.
Candidate sources: OpenStax, Contemporary Mathematics, §10.6 Area (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-contemporary-mathematics/blob/main/modules/m00130/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Calculus Volume 1, §1.1 Review of Functions (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-calculus-bundle/blob/main/modules/m53477/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Contemporary Mathematics, §10.4 Polygons, Perimeter, and Circumference (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-contemporary-mathematics/blob/main/modules/m00128/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §25.6 The Formation of the Galaxy (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59955/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Prealgebra 2e, §9.4 Use Properties of Rectangles, Triangles, and Trapezoids (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-prealgebra-bundle/blob/main/modules/m81332/index.cnxml)
Originality: A classic visual proof. Ours makes the wavy edge flatten as the slices thin, and calls the circumference 'the crust' so the width (half the crust) is easy to see.
Not used: Archimedes and the history of π (not sourced here); integration; the area of an ellipse.
