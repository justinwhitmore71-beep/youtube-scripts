Twice as wide, four times the pizza

1[HOOK] A pizza twice as wide has four times as much pizza.

2[MASCOT: Twice as wide, twice as much?] Shouldn't twice as wide mean twice as much?

3 Slice it thin and line the slices up, tips alternating.

4 Thinner slices get closer to a rectangle.

5 Its height is the radius; its width, half the crust, is pi times the radius.

6[EQUATION: A = πr × r = πr²] So the area is radius times pi times radius: pi r squared.

7 Double the radius and both sides double, so the area quadruples.

8[TAKEAWAY] That's the squared in pi r squared: size counts twice.

Unroll the rings

rings straightened into a triangle

1[HOOK] Double a circle's radius, and its area doesn't double; it quadruples.

2[MASCOT: Why four times?] Why four times, not two?

3 Peel the circle into thin rings and straighten each one.

4 They stack into a triangle: base two pi r, the outer crust, and height r.

5[EQUATION: A = ½ × 2πr × r = πr²] Half base times height: half of two pi r, times r, pi r squared.

6 Double r, and the triangle doubles in both base and height.

7[TAKEAWAY] Two doublings make four times the area: that's what the squared means.
