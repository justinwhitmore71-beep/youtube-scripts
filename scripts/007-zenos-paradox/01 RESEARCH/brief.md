# Brief: Zeno's paradox: infinitely many steps, one finite trip

Series: Brain Breakers
Idea: Going halfway, then half the rest, forever, is infinitely many steps, but the step lengths 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + ... add up to exactly 1, so the trip is finite; at steady speed the times add up the same way.
Misconception: Infinitely many steps must take forever, so (as Zeno argued) you could never arrive.
Hook angle: Crossing a room takes infinitely many steps, and you still arrive.
Narrowed to: Only the dichotomy (halving) paradox, with a geometric series. No Achilles and the tortoise, no philosophy of the continuum.
Key condition to say aloud: Steady walking speed (so each half-distance takes half the time).
Candidate sources: OpenStax, Introduction to Philosophy 2e, §4.2 Classical Philosophy (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-introduction-philosophy/blob/main/modules/m00021/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Calculus Volume 2, §5.2 Infinite Series (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-calculus-bundle/blob/main/modules/m53739/index.cnxml); APEX Calculus, §8.2 Infinite Series (Hartman et al., Virginia Military Institute) (https://github.com/APEXCalculus/APEXCalculus_Source/blob/master/text/08_Series.tex)
Originality: Usually told as a story with Achilles. Ours keeps one picture, a bar being filled half by half, and makes the shrinking GAP the reason the sum is exactly 1.
Not used: Whether the maths fully answers Zeno's philosophical point (debated by philosophers; no source opened here, so the script only claims the maths and that you arrive); Achilles and the tortoise; dates for Zeno.
