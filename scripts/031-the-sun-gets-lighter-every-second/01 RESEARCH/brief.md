# Brief: why the Sun loses 4 million tons every second

Series: One Equation
Idea: Energy and mass are linked by E = mc²; the Sun radiates about 4 × 10^26 joules every second, so it loses E / c² ≈ 4 billion kilograms (4 million tons) of mass per second, yet c² is so huge that this barely dents it.
Misconception: The Sun burns like a fire and its mass stays the same; light carries away energy but not mass.
Hook angle: The Sun gets about four million tons lighter every single second.
Narrowed to: E = mc² applied to the Sun's total output. No fusion reaction steps, no neutrinos.
Key condition to say aloud: 'Four million tons' is metric tons (4 billion kg), rounded.
Candidate sources: OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §16.2 Mass, Energy, and the Theory of Relativity (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59886/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Physics, §10.2 Consequences of Special Relativity (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-physics/blob/main/modules/m54282/index.cnxml); OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §28.6 Relativistic Energy (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42546/index.cnxml); OpenStax, University Physics Volume 3, §5.9 Relativistic Energy (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58572/index.cnxml); OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §15.1 The Structure and Composition of the Sun (Rice University) (https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59877/index.cnxml)
Originality: Usually E = mc² is shown with bombs or reactors. Ours divides the Sun's real output by c² on screen, so the viewer sees where the 4 billion kilograms come from.
Not used: Nuclear bomb comparisons; 'the Sun's lifetime is X billion years' (only an exercise answer here); fusion details (hydrogen to helium).
