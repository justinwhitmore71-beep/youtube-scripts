1[HOOK] The Sun gets about four million tons lighter every single second.

2[MASCOT: Where does it go?] Where does that mass go?

3 It leaves as sunlight: energy and mass are two forms of one thing.

4[EQUATION: E = m c²] E equals m c squared: energy is mass times light speed squared.

5 Each second it shines out four times ten to the twenty-six joules of energy.

6 Divide by c squared and that's four billion kilograms per second.

7[TAKEAWAY] c squared is huge, so this barely dents the Sun, which can shine for billions of years.

1[HOOK] Every second, sunlight carries away about four billion kilograms of the Sun.

2[MASCOT: Light weighs something?] Light can carry mass away?

3[EQUATION: E = m c²] Einstein's E equals m c squared says energy and mass come as a package.

4 One kilogram of mass matches nine times ten to the sixteen joules.

5 So pouring out four times ten to the twenty-six joules a second costs four billion kilograms.

6 That sounds huge, but the Sun can keep it up for billions of years.

7[TAKEAWAY] Sunlight is the Sun slowly turning its own mass into light.
