| c1 | Mass and energy are linked by E = mc² (rest energy): mass can be converted into energy, which is the source of the Sun's light. | [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 3, §5.9 Relativistic Energy (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58572/index.cnxml): "Rest energy of an object is E0 = mc2 . | c2 | The Sun gives out about 4 × 10^26 joules every second (about 4 × 10^26 watts). | [OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §28.6 Relativistic Energy (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42546/index.cnxml): "The Sun produces energy at a rate of 4.00×1026 W by the fusion of hydrogen." | [OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §15.1 The Structure and Composition of the Sun (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59877/index.cnxml): "Luminosity Solar constant × area of spherical surface 1 AU in radius 3.8 × 1026 W" | OK |
| c3 | So the Sun loses about 4 billion kg (about 4 million metric tons) of mass every second. | [OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §16.2 Mass, Energy, and the Theory of Relativity (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59886/index.cnxml): "the amount of energy radiated by the Sun could be produced by the complete conversion of about 4 million tons of matter into energy inside the Sun each second." | [OpenStax, Physics, §10.2 Consequences of Special Relativity (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-physics/blob/main/modules/m54282/index.cnxml): "The sun loses about 4 × 109 kg of mass every second." | OK |
| c4 | Even so, the Sun has enough mass to keep shining at this rate for billions of years. | [OpenStax, Astronomy 2e, §16.2 Mass, Energy, and the Theory of Relativity (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-astronomy/blob/main/modules/m59886/index.cnxml): "the Sun contains more than enough mass to destroy such huge amounts of matter and still continue shining at its present rate for billions of years" | [OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §28.6 Relativistic Energy (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42546/index.cnxml): "how long could it produce energy at its current rate? | c5 | 1 kg of mass corresponds to 9 × 10^16 joules (1 × c², with c = 3 × 10^8 m/s). | [OpenStax, University Physics Volume 3, §5.9 Relativistic Energy (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-university-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m58572/index.cnxml): "Rest energy of an object is E0 = mc2 ." | [OpenStax, College Physics 2e, §24.3 The Electromagnetic Spectrum (Rice University)](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-college-physics-bundle/blob/main/modules/m42444/index.cnxml): "where c=3.00×108m/s is the speed of light" | OK |

Notes: c3 derived check: m = E / c² = 4 × 10^26 / (3 × 10^8)² = 4 × 10^26 / 9 × 10^16 ≈ 4.4 × 10^9 kg per second (College Physics' exercise answer is 4.44 × 10^9 kg); with Astronomy's 3.8 × 10^26 W, 4.2 × 10^9 kg. 4 × 10^9 kg = 4 million metric tons. c4 source 2 is a textbook exercise answer (4.5 × 10^10 years under the exercise's assumptions), used only to support 'billions of years'. c5: 1 kg × (3 × 10^8 m/s)² = 9 × 10^16 J. Quotes from the OpenStax source files on GitHub; '1026' is 10^26 and '109' is 10^9 written without superscripts.
